What does "significant" mean?

Yearly climate data is noisy: any patch of years can drift up or down by chance. A trend is called statistically significant when it's too consistent to plausibly be luck.

TrendScope runs the Mann-Kendall test, the standard trend test in climate science. It checks whether later years are consistently higher (or lower) than earlier ones, and computes a p-value: the probability of seeing a trend this strong in pure noise. Below 0.05 (a 1-in-20 fluke), scientists call it significant.

The rate of change is estimated with Sen's slope (the median of all pairwise slopes, robust to outlier years), cross-checked by ordinary linear regression.
